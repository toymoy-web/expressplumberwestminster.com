At a Glance

How These Cities Differ

Every city we serve near Westminster has its own real housing history, and that history is the biggest single factor in what a plumbing job actually involves. A quick look at each:

Broomfield became its own consolidated city and county in 2001, but its oldest neighborhoods date to a 1950s subdivision called Broomfield Heights, sitting alongside much newer master-planned communities like Anthem Highlands.

Arvada has real 19th and early 20th century roots in its Olde Town historic district, giving it some of the oldest supply and sewer lines anywhere in our coverage area, alongside newer west-side development like Candelas.

Thornton grew almost overnight starting in 1953, when developer Sam Hoffman built roughly 300 homes in weeks, which means a real share of its original South Thornton core shares the same plumbing age today.

Northglenn was built essentially all at once too, a single 1959 master-planned community that Life magazine once called the most perfectly planned in America, incorporated as its own city a decade later in 1969.

Federal Heights is one of the smallest incorporated cities in the metro, under 1.8 square miles, with a working-class housing history dating to the 1920s and its main growth period running from 1950 to 1980.

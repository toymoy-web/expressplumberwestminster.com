A Clogged Drain That Keeps Coming Back

A single slow sink is usually a local clog, but a drain that backs up in more than one fixture at once, or that clears with a plunger and returns within a week, points to something further down the line rather than at the fixture itself.

We treat multi-fixture backups as an emergency call, since a main line clog can push sewage back into the lowest drain in the house if it's left alone.

Cabling vs. Camera-Verified Clearing

A standard cable clears the immediate blockage, which is the right call for a kitchen sink, bathroom sink, or tub drain. For a recurring clog or a main line, we run a camera first so we're clearing the actual cause, root intrusion, a bellied section of pipe, grease buildup, rather than just punching a hole through it and waiting for it to return.

Streets near West 76th Avenue and Lowell, close to where the original DeSpain farm sat in the 1870s, carry some of the oldest plumbing in the city.

DIY Attempts Before You Call

A plunger or a store-bought drain snake handles a genuinely simple clog in a sink or tub. Chemical drain cleaners are worth avoiding, though, since they can damage older galvanized or cast iron pipe and make the eventual professional clearing more difficult and dangerous to perform.

If a plunger doesn't clear it within a couple of attempts, or the clog is in more than one fixture, it's past the point where a DIY approach is likely to help.

Keeping Drains Clear Between Visits

A simple drain strainer in kitchen and shower drains catches most of what causes buildup over time, and it's a five-dollar fix that prevents a real share of future clog calls.

Running hot water for a minute after using a garbage disposal also helps flush grease through before it has a chance to cool and stick to the pipe wall.

What We Ask Before Arriving

Knowing which fixtures are affected, just one sink or multiple drains at once, helps us bring the right equipment on the first trip rather than needing to return for a camera or jetting setup.

A single-fixture clearing is usually done within 30 to 45 minutes. A main line job with a camera inspection typically runs closer to an hour to ninety minutes.

Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

  • Water backs up in more than one fixture
  • Gurgling from a drain when another fixture runs
  • A clog that returns within a few weeks of clearing
  • Slow drainage across the whole house
  • Sewage smell near a floor drain or cleanout

Common Questions

Drain Cleaning & Unclogging FAQ

Why does my clog keep coming back after cabling?

A cable clears whatever's directly in its path, but if the real cause is root intrusion or a section of pipe that's sagged and collects debris, the clog rebuilds in the same spot. A camera inspection tells us which one we're dealing with before we recommend hydro jetting or a repair.

Is a slow drain in one sink worth calling about?

A single slow drain is rarely an emergency, but it's worth addressing before it fully blocks, since a partial clog in a kitchen line especially tends to get worse with normal grease and food buildup rather than better.

Are chemical drain cleaners bad for my pipes?

Repeated use can damage older galvanized or PVC pipe over time, and they're genuinely dangerous to have in a line right before a plumber needs to work on it. We'd rather clear it mechanically.

A Boiler That Won't Hold Pressure or Heat

Boiler systems are less common than forced-air furnaces in newer Westminster construction but still run in a real share of the city's older homes, and they fail differently than a standard water heater does.

A boiler that's losing pressure between cycles, making a kettling or banging sound, or short-cycling on and off usually points to a specific, repairable cause rather than a dead system.

What We Check

We start with the pressure relief valve and expansion tank, since a boiler losing pressure is almost always leaking somewhere in the closed loop rather than the boiler itself failing outright, then move to the circulator pump and any zone valves if specific rooms aren't heating.

Seasonal Timing Matters

A boiler repair in the middle of winter carries more urgency than the same issue found during a spring or fall inspection, since a household without heat in a Front Range cold snap isn't a minor inconvenience.

We recommend a pre-season check each fall for any boiler system, since catching a slow pressure leak or a failing circulator pump before the coldest months avoids the exact kind of emergency call that's hardest to schedule quickly in January.

Annual Maintenance Worth Doing

Bleeding radiators or baseboard units at the start of each heating season clears trapped air that causes uneven heat and banging noises, and it's a five-minute task most homeowners can do themselves with the right key.

A professional check of the pressure, the expansion tank's precharge, and the circulator pump once a year catches small issues before they become a mid-winter no-heat call.

What a Boiler Visit Involves

We pressure-test the closed loop system as a standard first step on any boiler call, since that single check rules out or confirms the most common cause, a slow leak, before moving to more involved diagnostics.

Most single-component repairs, a valve, a circulator pump, a pressure switch, are completed same-visit, since these are common parts we carry rather than special-order items.

Gas vs. Electric Boiler Differences

A gas boiler adds ignition and venting components to the troubleshooting list that an electric system doesn't have, which is part of why we ask which type you have before dispatching so the right parts come along.

Electric boilers fail less often from venting issues but more often from element or contactor problems, a different but equally repairable category of fault.

Serving Westminster and Nearby

Boiler systems are more concentrated in Westminster's older housing near 80030 and 80221 than in newer construction further west, and that's where most of our boiler calls originate.

We also carry parts for older, less common boiler brands that a lot of shops won't touch, since Westminster's older housing stock still runs a real mix of legacy systems alongside newer, more standard equipment.

We'll also note the manufacturer's recommended service interval for your specific system during any visit, since that schedule varies more between boiler brands than most homeowners realize.

Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

  • Boiler pressure drops between heating cycles
  • Banging or kettling sound during operation
  • One or more zones not heating
  • Pilot light or ignition failure
  • Visible water around the base of the unit

Common Questions

Boiler Repair FAQ

Why does my boiler keep losing pressure?

A slow pressure loss almost always means a leak somewhere in the closed loop, at a fitting, the expansion tank, or a relief valve, not the boiler itself failing. We pressure-test the system to find exactly where before replacing parts.

Is it worth repairing an old boiler or replacing it entirely?

A boiler past 15 to 20 years with a major component failure, like the heat exchanger itself, is usually a better replacement candidate. A circulator pump or valve issue on a newer system is almost always worth repairing.

Water Heater Replacement Cost in Westminster, CO

☎ Call (303) 552-3896

A standard 40 to 50 gallon tank water heater replacement in Westminster typically runs $1,400 to $2,200 including labor and haul-away, while a tankless conversion runs $2,500 to $3,800 depending on whether new gas line sizing or venting is required.

What Actually Drives the Price

The unit itself is only part of the cost. Labor, code compliance items like an expansion tank if your setup doesn't already have one, and access all factor in, and a straightforward closet swap costs meaningfully less than a unit tucked behind finished drywall or requiring new venting.

Fuel type matters too. A gas-to-gas or electric-to-electric swap is simpler and cheaper than converting fuel types, which can require new gas line sizing or electrical work.

Tank vs. Tankless Cost Comparison

A standard tank costs less upfront and installs faster, usually the same day, but has a shorter typical lifespan, 8 to 12 years, and takes up permanent closet or garage space.

A tankless unit costs more upfront and sometimes requires venting or gas line upgrades, but lasts longer, 15 to 20 years with proper maintenance, and provides continuous hot water rather than a limited tank capacity.

Hidden Costs to Ask About Upfront

An expansion tank, required by code in many setups and often not included in a bare quote, typically adds $150 to $300. Permit fees, which we always include and pull as part of the job, are sometimes left off a lowball quote elsewhere. And haul-away of the old unit should be included, not billed separately.

Financing and Timing

Water heater failure is rarely convenient, and same-day replacement is possible for most standard tank swaps when the unit is in stock. A tankless conversion needing gas or venting work is more often scheduled a day or two out to source any needed parts.

A Real Westminster Replacement, Start to Finish

A typical call: a homeowner with a 12-year-old 40-gallon tank notices lukewarm water that never fully heats up. On inspection, the heating element has failed and the tank itself shows early rust at a seam, past the point where replacing just the element makes sense.

We quote the replacement on the spot, same brand and size or a suggested upgrade if the household's hot water demand has grown, and in most cases complete the swap that same day, well within the phone-quote-to-hot-water-restored window homeowners actually care about.

Financing and Comparing Quotes

When comparing quotes, confirm each one includes the permit, haul-away, and any code-required expansion tank, since a lower number that leaves those out isn't actually a lower total cost, just a deferred one.

We price the unit and labor separately in every quote specifically so it's easy to compare against another company's number apples to apples, rather than a bundled figure that's hard to evaluate.

Rebates and Efficiency Considerations

Some high-efficiency water heaters, particularly heat pump models, qualify for utility or federal rebates that can offset a meaningful part of the upfront cost, though eligibility and rebate amounts change over time, so it's worth checking current programs before assuming a specific number.

A higher-efficiency unit typically costs more upfront but less to operate month to month, which is worth factoring into a real cost comparison rather than looking at the installation price alone.

Questions to Ask Before You Sign a Quote

Ask exactly what's included: the unit itself, labor, permit, haul-away, and any code-required parts like an expansion tank. A quote that's meaningfully lower than others is often missing one of these, not actually a better deal once the full scope is accounted for.

Ask about warranty coverage on both the unit and the labor separately, since manufacturer warranties on parts and a contractor's own labor warranty are two different things, and a low price sometimes comes with a shorter or nonexistent labor warranty.

Ask how soon the work can actually be scheduled, especially if you're currently without hot water. A same-day or next-day installation matters more to most households than a marginal price difference, and it's worth confirming realistic timing upfront rather than assuming.

A water heater failure rarely happens at a convenient time, and having a rough sense of real costs beforehand means you're making a decision, not just reacting under pressure when the quote arrives. We're glad to walk through your specific situation and give you real numbers before you're stuck without hot water and feeling like you have to say yes to whatever's in front of you.

It's also worth asking whether your existing unit is still under manufacturer warranty before assuming a full replacement is the only option. Some tank warranties run 6 to 12 years on the tank itself, and a warranty claim can meaningfully offset replacement cost even when the unit needs to be swapped rather than repaired.

Whatever you decide, don't let a failed water heater sit unaddressed for long. Beyond the obvious inconvenience, a failing unit that's leaking, even slowly, risks water damage to the surrounding area the longer it's left in place.

Real numbers, given honestly upfront, are worth more than a lowball estimate that grows once work has already started. That's the standard we hold ourselves to on every quote, whether it's the number you were hoping to hear or not, and it's a standard worth expecting from anyone you hire for a job this significant to your home.

Keep this page bookmarked for the next time a water heater question comes up, whether it's this year or years from now, since these numbers hold up reasonably well as a general reference over time.

Common Questions

FAQ

How long does a water heater replacement take?

A standard tank-to-tank swap is usually a same-day job, typically 2 to 3 hours. A tankless conversion needing new gas line sizing or venting can take a full day.

Is it worth repairing my water heater instead of replacing it?

A unit under 8 years old with a single failed part, a thermocouple or heating element, is usually worth repairing. A unit past 10 to 12 years old that's leaking from the tank body itself is past the point of a cost-effective repair.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Westminster?

Yes, Westminster requires a permit for water heater replacement, and we pull it as part of every installation rather than treating it as optional.

What size water heater do I need?

A 40-gallon tank suits most 2 to 3 person households. A larger household or one with a soaking tub often needs 50 gallons or a tankless unit sized for simultaneous fixture use.

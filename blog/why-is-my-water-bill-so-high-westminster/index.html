Why Is My Water Bill So High in Westminster, CO?

☎ Call (303) 552-3896

The most common causes of a sudden water bill spike in Westminster are a running toilet, an underground leak between the meter and the house, a slab leak, a dripping or stuck-open irrigation valve, and, less often, a genuine change in household water use, and checking the water meter with everything off is the fastest way to tell which category you're dealing with.

Start at the Meter

Most Westminster water meters have a small leak indicator, often a red or silver triangle or dial, that moves with any flow at all. Turn off every fixture and appliance in the house, then watch the meter for a minute or two. If it's moving, water is flowing somewhere you're not seeing it. This is the standard first check any utility or plumber recommends before assuming a leak requires professional detection equipment.

Cause One: A Running Toilet

A worn flapper or fill valve is the single most common cause of a silent, ongoing water bill increase, since a toilet can run continuously without making enough noise to notice. Drop a little food coloring in the tank and check the bowl 15 minutes later without flushing. Color in the bowl means it's leaking.

Cause Two: An Underground or Slab Leak

If the meter is moving but nothing inside the house explains it, the leak is likely between the meter and the house, or under the slab itself. This isn't a DIY diagnosis. It requires professional leak detection equipment to locate without guessing where to dig or open concrete.

Cause Three: Irrigation

A stuck-open sprinkler valve or a cracked irrigation line can run a significant amount of water without ever showing up inside the house, especially if the system runs on a schedule you're not always home to notice.

Cause Four: A Real Change in Use

Sometimes the explanation is genuinely just more water use, a house guest staying longer, a new appliance, filling a pool. Compare the current bill to the same month last year rather than just last month, since seasonal irrigation use skews month-to-month comparisons.

A Real Diagnosis Walkthrough

A homeowner called after their bill jumped by roughly $80 in one month. The meter test showed movement with everything off. A dye test on both toilets came back negative. Detection equipment traced the sound to a section of yard near an irrigation valve, not the house itself, a cracked line feeding one zone of the sprinkler system.

The fix was a straightforward irrigation line repair, well outside what the homeowner had assumed was a slab leak based on the water bill alone, which is exactly why working through the checklist in order matters before assuming the most expensive explanation.

What to Have Ready When You Call

Your last two or three water bills, whether the meter moved during your own test, and whether you have an irrigation system all help us narrow things down before a technician even arrives, which can mean bringing the right equipment on the first visit instead of a second trip.

Comparing Your Bill to Neighbors

Westminster's water rates are tiered, meaning usage above a certain threshold is billed at a higher rate per gallon, so a leak that pushes a household into a higher tier can cause a bill increase that's disproportionate to the actual extra water used.

Comparing your bill to a similarly sized neighboring household, if you're comfortable asking, can be a useful sanity check, though your own year-over-year comparison remains the more reliable signal overall.

If You've Ruled Out the Obvious Causes

If the meter isn't moving with everything off, the toilet dye tests come back negative, and irrigation isn't the explanation, the increase is likely a genuine change in household water use rather than a leak, worth confirming by comparing the current bill against the same month a year ago rather than just last month.

If the meter is moving and none of the visible checks explain it, that's the point to call for professional leak detection rather than continuing to guess, since the leak is likely somewhere between the meter and the house, or under the slab, neither of which is diagnosable without the right equipment.

Acting quickly matters here beyond just stopping the cost. An undetected leak, especially a slab leak, can cause real structural and mold issues the longer it's allowed to continue unaddressed.

A water bill spike is frustrating, but it's almost always explainable once you work through the checklist in order rather than jumping straight to the most expensive assumption. Most of the time, the actual cause is simpler and cheaper to fix than homeowners initially fear.

If you've checked everything on this list and the increase still doesn't have a clear explanation, don't keep guessing indefinitely. A professional leak detection visit is a relatively small cost compared to months of an unexplained bill increase, and it gives you a definitive answer rather than continued uncertainty.

Keep a rough record of your bills over time if you don't already, even just a simple note of the total each month. That history makes spotting a genuine anomaly, versus normal seasonal variation, much easier the next time something looks off.

A high water bill is stressful, but it's rarely mysterious once you actually work through it systematically. Start at the meter, work through the checklist, and you'll almost always land on a clear answer well before you need to guess or assume the worst-case scenario is automatically what's happening.

And if you ever need a second opinion on what the meter is telling you, we're a phone call away, any time of day, for a straightforward answer rather than a sales pitch.

Common Questions

FAQ

How much water can a running toilet actually waste?

A running toilet can waste hundreds of gallons a day depending on the severity, which adds up to a real, visible jump on a monthly bill even though it's completely silent to most people.

Can Westy Water tell me if there's a leak?

The utility can sometimes flag unusually high usage on your account, but they don't locate the leak itself. That's a job for a plumber with detection equipment.

Is a high water bill always a plumbing problem?

No. Compare month-over-month and year-over-year before assuming a leak. Irrigation season alone can explain a real seasonal increase without anything being wrong.

How fast should I act on a suspected leak?

Once you've confirmed the meter is moving with everything off, don't wait. An undetected leak keeps costing money and, if it's a slab leak, can cause real damage the longer it runs.

Seeing the Problem Before We Touch the Yard

A camera inspection runs a waterproof camera on a flexible cable through the sewer line, giving us a live view of the pipe's actual condition rather than guessing based on symptoms alone.

It's the right first step before any sewer repair, before buying a home with an older sewer connection, or when a line has clogged more than once in the same area.

What We Look For

Root intrusion, cracked or offset joints, bellied sections that collect standing water and debris, and in older parts of the city, deteriorating clay or Orangeburg pipe that's reached the end of its service life.

What You Get After the Inspection

We provide a straightforward rundown of what the camera found, in plain language, along with real recommendations if something needs attention, not just a video file with no explanation attached.

If the line is in good shape, that's worth knowing too, and we'll tell you that plainly rather than manufacturing a problem to justify the visit.

What the Footage Actually Shows

The camera reveals pipe material, joint condition, root intrusion location and severity, any bellied or offset sections, and the general state of the line's interior surface, all details that guesswork from symptoms alone can't provide.

We record the footage and can walk you through it after the fact too, not just narrate it live, so you have something concrete to reference if you're getting a second opinion.

Scheduling and What to Expect

We ask for access to the nearest cleanout when scheduling, since that's typically the fastest entry point for the camera rather than pulling a toilet or opening a wall.

A standard inspection, cleanout to the property line, usually takes about 30 to 45 minutes, with the footage available to review the same day.

Why We Recommend It Before Buying or Selling

A sewer line issue is one of the more expensive surprises a home inspection can miss entirely, since standard inspections don't typically include a camera run through the sewer lateral.

For sellers, a clean inspection report is a genuine selling point worth having in hand before listing. For buyers, it's a small cost that can prevent a five-figure surprise after closing.

Serving Westminster and Nearby

Inspection requests come most often from 80030 and 80221 ahead of a repair decision, and increasingly from buyers doing pre-purchase due diligence across every ZIP we cover.

We keep inspection footage on file for at least a year, which is useful if a related issue comes up later or if you need to reference the findings for an insurance or real estate conversation.

We can also provide a written summary alongside the footage for real estate transactions specifically, formatted the way most agents and inspectors expect to see it attached to a disclosure packet. If the line does need follow-up work, that same footage carries directly into the repair quote instead of needing a second inspection. Ask for the footage in whatever format your specific transaction requires, and we'll make sure it's delivered that way.

Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

  • Recurring clogs in the same drain or area
  • Buying a home with an older sewer line
  • A sewer line that's never been inspected
  • Preparing for a landscaping project near the line
  • Unexplained wet or sunken area in the yard

Common Questions

Sewer Camera Inspection FAQ

Should I get a camera inspection before buying a home?

It's one of the more overlooked pre-purchase checks, especially on a home built before the 1980s where clay sewer pipe is common. A camera inspection can catch a five-figure repair before it becomes your problem instead of the seller's.

Can I be there while the inspection happens?

Yes, and we'd actually recommend it. Seeing the footage in real time, with us narrating what we're looking at, tends to make the findings clearer than a written summary alone.

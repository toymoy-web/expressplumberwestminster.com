Warm Spot on the Floor or a Water Bill That Won't Add Up?

A warm patch on an otherwise cool floor, or a water bill that's climbed with no visible leak anywhere in the house, is one of the more common ways a slab leak shows up before anyone realizes that's what it is.

Why Slab Leaks Happen

Copper supply lines run under the concrete slab in a lot of Westminster's older single-story construction, and over decades, ground movement, corrosion, or a manufacturing flaw in the original pipe can wear a pinhole leak that's completely invisible until water starts finding its way up.

Westminster's 14-story City Hall Bell Tower, a 1986 tribute to London's Big Ben, sits in the older core of town where a lot of the original 1960s and 1970s housing stock still runs its first set of supply lines.

Detection First, Then a Real Choice Between Repair and Reroute

We confirm and pinpoint the leak with acoustic equipment before any concrete is touched, then walk through two real options: a spot repair that opens a small, targeted section of slab directly over the leak, or a reroute that runs new line through the attic or an exterior wall, bypassing the slab section entirely.

The right choice usually comes down to the age of the rest of the home's piping. One confirmed slab leak on an otherwise sound system often favors a spot repair. A home already showing other corrosion signs often favors a reroute or a broader repipe conversation. Either approach is completed to the Colorado Plumbing Code (IPC).

Insurance and Slab Leaks

Many homeowner policies cover the cost of accessing and repairing a slab leak, though coverage specifics vary by insurer and policy, so it's worth a call to your provider once the leak is confirmed.

We provide documentation of the detection and repair that's typically useful for a claim either way, regardless of how your specific policy handles it.

Why Some Slab Leaks Go Undetected for Months

A slow leak under a slab can take months to produce an obvious sign, especially in a section of the home that's less trafficked, which is part of why a climbing water bill is often the first real clue rather than anything visible.

Checking the meter periodically, even without a specific concern, is a low-effort habit that catches a slab leak faster than waiting for a warm spot or a bill spike to prompt the check.

From Detection to Repair Decision

We complete detection and give you real repair options in the same visit whenever possible, rather than requiring a separate appointment just to hear the results.

Once a repair approach is chosen, spot repairs are often scheduled within a few days. A full reroute takes more planning time to map the new line's path.

Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

  • Warm spot on the floor with no obvious source
  • Unexplained spike in the water bill
  • Sound of running water with everything off
  • Cracking in flooring or baseboards
  • Damp carpet or flooring with no visible leak

Common Questions

Slab Leak Detection & Repair FAQ

Is a slab leak always an emergency?

A confirmed slab leak should be addressed quickly since ongoing water under a foundation can affect the slab itself and encourage mold, but a brief same-day or next-day scheduling window is usually fine once the main water is shut off if needed.

Can a slab leak cause structural damage if left too long?

Prolonged water under a foundation can affect soil stability beneath the slab in some cases, which is part of why we treat a confirmed slab leak as worth addressing promptly rather than indefinitely postponing.

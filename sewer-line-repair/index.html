Backups, Slow Drains, and Sewer Odors

A sewer line problem usually shows up first as a slow drain that affects the whole house rather than one fixture, sometimes paired with gurgling from toilets or a sewage smell near a floor drain or cleanout.

We treat an active backup into the home as an emergency, since sewage in a living space is a health issue on top of the plumbing problem itself.

Camera Diagnosis Before Any Digging

Every sewer line repair starts with a camera inspection, which tells us exactly what we're dealing with: root intrusion, a bellied or sagging section, a cracked joint, or in older sections of the city, deteriorating clay pipe.

That diagnosis determines whether the fix is a targeted spot repair, trenchless lining that avoids digging up a yard entirely, or in more severe cases, a full line replacement. Any excavation or lining work is permitted and inspected to the Colorado Plumbing Code (IPC-based) rather than done off the books.

Trenchless Options for Older Sections of the City

Trenchless lining works well on a structurally sound pipe that's cracked or root-damaged but hasn't collapsed, essentially installing a new pipe inside the old one without excavating the yard.

A severely bellied or collapsed section still needs traditional excavation, which is exactly why the camera inspection happens before we recommend either approach rather than defaulting to the more expensive option.

Preventing Root Intrusion Going Forward

Trees planted near a sewer line's path are the root cause, often literally, of recurring intrusion, and root-killing treatments poured down a cleanout on a schedule can meaningfully slow regrowth between full repairs.

We'll flag which specific trees on a property are the likely source during a camera inspection, which is useful information even beyond the immediate repair.

What Happens After the Camera Run

We review the footage with you on-site immediately after the camera inspection, pointing out exactly what we found, before recommending a specific repair approach or price.

A confirmed spot repair is often scheduled within a day or two once approved. A full replacement typically takes more lead time to schedule equipment and any required permits.

Materials We Use for Repairs

Trenchless lining uses a cured-in-place epoxy liner that effectively creates a new pipe inside the old one. Traditional spot repairs typically use PVC matched to the existing line's diameter.

We explain which material and method applies to your specific situation during the camera review, rather than defaulting to whichever approach is easiest for us to schedule.

Serving Westminster and Nearby

Sewer line repairs cluster in the mature, tree-lined blocks of 80030 and 80221, where root intrusion into aging clay pipe is the single most common cause we see.

We also check the connection at the property line during any sewer repair, since a problem right at that boundary sometimes involves the utility rather than being solely the homeowner's responsibility to fix.

Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

  • Multiple drains backing up at once
  • Gurgling from toilets when other fixtures run
  • Sewage smell near a cleanout or floor drain
  • Sinkhole or soggy patch in the yard
  • Sewage backing up into the lowest drain in the home

Common Questions

Sewer Line Repair FAQ

Do you have to dig up my whole yard?

Not always. A confirmed spot repair or a trenchless lining can often fix the affected section without excavating the entire line, though a severely damaged or collapsed section may still require open-trench work.

What causes most sewer line problems in Westminster?

Root intrusion from mature trees is the single most common cause we see, followed by aging clay pipe in the older sections of the city and, less often, a bellied section that's settled over decades and collects debris.

How do I know if it's my line or the city's?

The property line is generally the dividing point. We determine this during the camera inspection and can tell you clearly which side of that line the problem falls on before recommending a repair.

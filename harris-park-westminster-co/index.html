About Harris Park

Harris Park is the part of Westminster where the city began. The City of Westminster's Harris Park Community Vision Plan, published in 2021, traces the area to a first settler farm in 1863. It also names C.J. Harris, who arrived in 1881 soon after the railroad and consolidated land to resell to fruit farmers. In 1911 the Town of Harris voted to incorporate as the Town of Westminster.

The plan describes a core generally bounded by West 72nd Avenue on the south, Irving Street on the east, 74th Avenue on the north and Bradburn Boulevard on the west. A larger surrounding neighborhood runs north toward 80th Avenue and US 36. Treat those streets as anchors, because the plan's own maps show the exact lines. The Bowles House, a red brick home at West 72nd Avenue and Newton Street, is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

What the housing mix looks like

According to the plan, buildings in the core date from the 1910s onward. They are one and two stories, and some have been reused for new purposes. Beyond the core the area is primarily single-family detached homes, and the larger neighborhood also includes duplexes and small apartment buildings.

That mix is the practical point for plumbing. A house of any age can have original pipe, newer pipe, or both. A duplex or small apartment building adds a question a single house does not, which is where one unit's water and drain lines end and the next begin.

We do not guess what is behind a wall from a street name. The water heater label, the visible supply lines, the cleanout and whatever history you can share tell us far more, and we recommend from what we find.

Older buildings and renovation work

The vision plan also calls for identifying historic resources and keeping them part of the neighborhood. It names The Grange and a building at 3915 West 73rd Avenue as examples, and it says a map of historic resources is available at city offices.

If you own an older building, tell the plumber which finishes you want to protect before work begins, such as original plaster, tile or flooring. Camera inspections and leak locators reduce how much has to be opened. Renovation in an old building can also uncover earlier repairs, so ask for photos of anything found.

Services Harris Park homeowners ask about

When more than one fixture drains slowly or a floor drain gurgles, the usual first step is drain cleaning. If the problem keeps coming back, a sewer camera inspection shows the inside of the line before anyone digs. A stained ceiling, a damp wall or a water bill that jumps points toward leak detection.

A water heater that is rusting at the base or running out of hot water quickly is a water heater repair or replacement conversation. Homes with repeated pipe leaks may also want to talk through repiping options before the next repair.

If you own a duplex or a small apartment building, tell the dispatcher how many units there are and which fixtures are affected. That helps the right equipment arrive the first time. Larger building systems fall under commercial plumbing.

Check your water line before you need it

The City of Westminster publishes a Service Line Inventory map. Enter your address, find the dot in front of your home and click it to see meter and service line information. The city owns the line from the water main to the meter pit, and the customer owns the line from the meter into the building.

The city reports that the lines it has identified so far are copper and notes that some older homes may have galvanized steel lines. If your line shows as unknown, the city asks residents to call 303-658-2462. Knowing what you have makes any later repair conversation simpler.

Sources: City of Westminster, Harris Park Community Vision Plan (2021) and City of Westminster, Service Line Inventory.

Common Questions

Harris Park Plumbing FAQ

Where is Harris Park in Westminster?

The city's Harris Park Community Vision Plan describes a core around West 72nd Avenue, with Irving Street, 74th Avenue and Bradburn Boulevard as general edges, and a larger neighborhood reaching toward 80th Avenue and US 36. The plan's maps show the exact lines. Addresses here are commonly listed under the 80030 ZIP code.

Do older Harris Park homes have galvanized pipes?

Some may, but it depends on the individual house and its repair history. The City of Westminster notes that some older homes may have galvanized steel service lines. The city's service line map is a good first check, and a plumber can confirm what is visible at the meter, the water heater and under the sinks.

What should I tell the dispatcher about a duplex or small apartment building?

Say how many units the building has, which fixtures are affected and whether you know where the shutoff valves are. If a leak might involve a shared wall or a shared line, mention that too, so the visit is planned around it.

My drain keeps backing up. Is it the sewer line?

A single slow sink is often local to that fixture. Several fixtures backing up together, or a floor drain that gurgles, can point to the main line. A camera inspection shows the cause instead of leaving it to guesswork.

No Hot Water Right Now?

A water heater that's gone cold, is leaking at the base, or is making a rumbling or popping sound is one of the most common emergency calls we run in Westminster, and most of the time it's a fixable part, not a dead tank.

A failed thermocouple, a tripped high-limit switch, or a bad heating element on an electric unit are all same-visit repairs. A tank that's actively leaking from the base, rather than from a fitting, is the one situation where repair usually isn't the right call.

What We Check First

We start at the shutoff and the thermostat setting, since those account for more "no hot water" calls than most homeowners expect, then move to the thermocouple or heating element, the pressure relief valve, and the anode rod.

Repair vs. Replace

A tank under 8 years old with a bad element, thermocouple, or gas valve is almost always worth repairing. A tank past 10 to 12 years that's leaking from the tank body itself, rather than a fitting, has usually corroded from the inside and needs replacing rather than repairing.

How Long a Repair Actually Takes

Most thermocouple, element, or gas valve repairs are a one-visit job, typically an hour to ninety minutes once the part is confirmed. A control board replacement on a newer unit can take longer since some boards need to be special-ordered rather than carried on the truck.

We carry the most common parts for the major brands sold in this area, which is why most repairs finish same-day rather than requiring a second visit.

Simple Checks Before You Call

Confirm the thermostat is set correctly and the breaker or gas valve hasn't tripped or been bumped closed, since both are common, easily fixed causes that don't require a service visit at all.

If the unit is gas, check whether the pilot is lit and visible through the small window most tanks have. If it's out and won't relight following the label's instructions, that's the point to call rather than keep trying.

Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

  • No hot water or lukewarm water only
  • Rumbling, popping, or banging from the tank
  • Water pooling at the base of the unit
  • Pilot light won't stay lit
  • Discolored or rusty hot water

Common Questions

Water Heater Repair FAQ

Is a leaking water heater always an emergency?

A leak at a fitting or the drain valve can usually wait for a same-day appointment once the water is shut off. A leak from the tank body itself means the tank is failing and should be treated as an emergency, since it can worsen quickly.

Why does my pilot light keep going out?

Most repeat pilot-light failures trace back to a weak thermocouple, which is a low-cost part that stops signaling the gas valve to stay open. A dirty flue or a draft near the unit can cause the same symptom, which is why we check airflow before replacing parts.

Will my homeowner's insurance cover a water heater repair?

Standard wear-and-tear repairs generally aren't covered. Sudden failures that cause water damage, like a tank rupturing, may trigger coverage for the resulting damage, though the unit replacement itself typically isn't included.

Water Bill Climbing but Can't Find the Leak?

If your meter is spinning with everything in the house turned off, or your bill has jumped and nothing's visibly wrong, what you need is professional leak detection, electronic listening equipment that pinpoints a leak without tearing open walls or slab to go looking for it.

How Electronic Leak Detection Actually Works

We start at the meter with everything in the house shut off. If it's still moving, the leak is somewhere between the meter and the fixtures, which narrows the search before any equipment comes out.

From there we use acoustic listening equipment that picks up the sound of water escaping under pressure, even through concrete or behind drywall, and pinpoint the location within a small radius rather than guessing.

The Most Common Leak Locations in Westminster Homes

Slab leaks show up most in the older housing stock near the Bell Tower area, where copper supply lines run under the foundation rather than through an accessible crawl space.

Newer homes closer to Orchard Town Center see fewer slab leaks and more failures at fittings, water heater connections, and irrigation lines instead.

What Happens After the Leak Is Found

Once we've pinpointed the leak, we walk through the actual repair options on the spot, a spot repair, a reroute, or in the case of a fitting or appliance connection, a straightforward part swap, with real pricing for each before any work begins.

We don't charge extra to explain the trade-offs. The detection visit itself gives you the information either way, even if you decide to get a second opinion on the repair itself.

Reading Your Water Meter Yourself

Most Westminster water meters have a small leak indicator, often a red or silver triangle or dial, that moves with any flow at all. Watching it for a minute with everything off is a genuinely useful first check before calling.

A meter that's completely still but a bill that's still climbed points toward a different explanation, sometimes a leak that's intermittent, sometimes simply a change in household water use worth checking first.

Setting Up the Detection Visit

Having recent water bills on hand, even a rough idea of when the increase started, helps narrow the timeline before equipment is even used, and it's worth digging those up before we arrive.

A standard detection visit runs 60 to 90 minutes depending on the property's size and how many potential sources need to be ruled out along the way.

Serving Westminster and Nearby

Leak detection dispatches most often to 80003, 80005, and 80020, where a mix of housing ages means both slab leaks and fitting failures show up regularly.

Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

  • Water meter moves with everything off
  • Unexplained spike in the water bill
  • A warm spot on the floor with no obvious cause
  • Sound of running water with no fixture on
  • Mold or musty smell with no visible source

Common Questions

Leak Detection FAQ

How accurate is electronic leak detection?

Modern acoustic equipment typically narrows a leak to within a foot or two before any digging or wall opening starts, which is the entire point, it turns a guess-and-open-walls job into a targeted repair.

What if the leak is under my slab?

A confirmed slab leak has two real fixes: a spot repair that opens a small section of slab directly over the leak, or a reroute that runs a new line through the attic or an exterior wall and avoids the slab entirely. We'll walk you through both with real cost numbers before any work starts.

Do I need to be home for a leak detection visit?

It's helpful but not always required if we have clear access to the meter and the general area of concern. We'll walk through what we found either way once the visit is complete.

About Sheridan Green

Sheridan Green is a residential neighborhood in central Westminster along the US 36 corridor. A homes.com guide places it in ZIP code 80020 and counts about 2,600 homes with a median build year of 1986. It describes ranch, split-level and contemporary houses, many from the 1960s and 1970s and some from the 1990s, with large fenced backyards.

The neighborhood has a recent civic story. Jeffco Public Schools closed Sheridan Green Elementary after the 2022 to 2023 school year, and ownership of the site moved to the City of Westminster in fall 2023. City Council approved $3.7 million to remove the building and expand the adjacent park from 6 to 12 acres. On October 27, 2025, Council approved renaming Stratford Park to Sheridan Green Park.

The city says deconstruction finished on October 14, 2025 and that park construction began in January 2026 with completion estimated for late 2026. Check the city's Sheridan Green page for current status. The Sheridan Green Trail, about 1.5 miles along Westminster Boulevard, runs from the Broomfield city limits to the Westminster Promenade.

Homes of different ages on the same streets

A neighborhood built over several decades means two houses on the same block can be a generation apart. One may have had its plumbing updated more than once, and the other may be largely as built. We do not assume which is which. Water heater labels, visible supply lines and repair history say more than a subdivision name.

Buying or selling in an established neighborhood

If you are buying or selling a house here, a sewer camera inspection and a water heater check give you something documented to work from. Keep records of past plumbing repairs, because they answer the age question better than the year the house was built.

Backyards, hose bibs and sprinklers

Large grass yards usually mean outdoor faucets and often a sprinkler system. Westminster's own guidance names sprinkler systems among the most common hidden leak sources, so it is worth checking them early in the season. Our leak detection service finds the source of a hidden leak without tearing up the yard.

Before the first hard freeze, disconnect hoses and shut off and drain outdoor lines as the installer directs. If a pipe does freeze, our frozen pipe repair page explains what to do first.

Check your water line before you need it

The City of Westminster owns the water line from the main to the meter pit, and the customer owns the line from the meter into the building. The city publishes a Service Line Inventory map where you can enter your address and click the dot in front of your home to see meter and service line information. If your line shows as unknown, the city asks residents to call 303-658-2462.

The city reports that the lines it has identified so far are copper and notes that some older homes may have galvanized steel lines. Knowing what you have makes any later repair conversation simpler, and it is free to look.

Everyday repairs

Most calls from established neighborhoods are ordinary ones. A slow drain, a running toilet, a dripping faucet or an aging water heater are all common. The toilet repair and water heater repair pages describe the usual fixes so you know what to expect before you call.

If you are near the Broomfield line, our Broomfield page covers that side of the border.

Common Questions

Sheridan Green Plumbing FAQ

What is happening with the old Sheridan Green Elementary site?

Jeffco closed the school after the 2022 to 2023 school year, and the City of Westminster took ownership in fall 2023. The city says the building has been removed and that the adjacent park is being expanded from 6 to 12 acres, with construction under way and completion estimated for late 2026. The city's Sheridan Green page has current details.

Is the Sheridan Green Trail the same as the neighborhood?

No. The trail is a roughly 1.5-mile path along Westminster Boulevard between the Broomfield city limits and the Westminster Promenade. It shares the name, but it is a trail, not a boundary.

How do I tell if I have a hidden leak?

Westminster's utility sends a Possible Leak Alert when a meter records continuous water use for 24 hours straight, and the city's guidance says that pattern usually means a leak. Toilets and sprinkler systems are the common hidden sources. If you cannot find it, a leak locator can.

How do I protect outdoor faucets and sprinklers in winter?

Disconnect hoses before the first hard freeze, shut off the supply to outdoor faucets if your home has an interior shutoff, and have sprinkler lines drained as the installer directs. If a pipe freezes, shut off the water at the main and call before attempting to thaw it with open flame.

What should I do first if my water heater starts leaking?

Turn off the cold water supply valve on the heater, then switch off its power at the breaker or set a gas unit to pilot. Open a hot tap somewhere in the house to relieve pressure. Keep towels and a bucket handy and call so the unit can be checked. A slow drip at a fitting can often be repaired, while a leak from the tank itself usually means replacement.

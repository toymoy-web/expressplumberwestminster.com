Snaked It and It's Already Clogging Again?

If a cable has already been run through your line and the clog came right back within a few weeks, the actual fix is usually hydro jetting, high-pressure water that scours the full diameter of the pipe clean rather than punching a single hole through the blockage.

Why Jetting Clears What a Cable Can't

A cable clears a path through whatever's blocking the line, but it leaves grease, scale, and root hairs coating the rest of the pipe wall, which is exactly what the next clog builds on.

A hydro jet uses pressurized water through a specialized nozzle to scour the entire inner surface of the pipe, not just punch through the middle, which is why it holds up longer against recurring clogs.

When Jetting Is and Isn't the Right Call

Jetting works well on grease buildup, scale, and moderate root intrusion. It's not the right tool for a pipe that's already cracked, collapsed, or significantly offset, since high pressure in a compromised line can make things worse rather than better, which is why we camera the line first.

What to Expect During the Service

A jetting visit typically takes 45 minutes to about two hours depending on the line's length and how much buildup has accumulated, and we run a follow-up camera pass afterward to confirm the line is actually clear rather than just assuming it based on flow.

Homes on well-maintained PVC lines usually see the cleanest results. Older clay or cast iron sections sometimes reveal a structural issue during jetting that a cable would never have surfaced.

How Jetting Compares on Cost Over Time

A single jetting visit costs more upfront than a cable clearing, but a line that keeps re-clogging every few months on cabling alone often costs more in repeat service calls over a year or two than one thorough jetting would have.

We're upfront when a line's history suggests jetting is the more economical choice long-term, rather than defaulting to the cheaper visit every single time.

Scheduling a Jetting Visit

We recommend a camera inspection either right before or as part of the same appointment, so the jetting decision is based on what's actually in the line rather than an assumption from symptoms alone.

A typical residential jetting appointment, including the camera check, runs 90 minutes to two hours depending on the line's length and condition.

Equipment We Run

Our jetting equipment runs at commercial-grade pressure calibrated to the pipe material and diameter involved, since running full pressure through an older or narrower line without adjustment risks damaging rather than cleaning it.

We size the nozzle and pressure to the specific job rather than using one setting for every line, which matters more on the mixed pipe ages common across Westminster's housing stock.

Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

  • Multiple slow drains across the house
  • Grease buildup in kitchen lines
  • Root intrusion confirmed by camera
  • Recurring sewer backups

Common Questions

Hydro Jetting FAQ

Is hydro jetting safe for older pipes?

It depends on the pipe's actual condition, not just its age. A camera inspection first tells us whether the line can handle the pressure or whether a section needs repair before jetting is attempted.

How often does a line need jetting?

A line with a history of grease buildup or root intrusion often benefits from jetting every 1 to 2 years as maintenance, rather than waiting for a full clog to happen again.

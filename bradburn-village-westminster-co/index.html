About Bradburn Village

Bradburn Village is a master-planned, mixed-use community along West 120th Avenue in north Westminster, near the Broomfield border. A Urban Land Institute case study describes it as a 123-acre new urbanist project with 16 acres of open space. Work started in 2001, the village opened in 2002 and the developer was Continuum Partners.

The planned mix was more than 800 residences. That included 275 single-family homes, 150 townhouses, 310 rental apartments and 33 live/work units, plus 108 apartments above retail. The plan also included about 154,830 square feet of retail and 29,000 square feet of office space. Those are planned totals, and the finished community may differ.

A Westword article describes apartments and townhomes closest to 120th Avenue, larger single-family homes toward the rear, and garages behind the houses.

Why a mixed community changes the plumbing questions

In a neighborhood like this, a single street can include a detached house, an attached townhome and an apartment above a shop. Each has a different answer to the question of who is responsible for what. Before you call about a leak, find out whether the line in question is yours, shared with neighbors or the association's.

Attached homes share walls, and water can travel. If a leak starts, shut off your own supply first, then check the units around you if you can do so safely. A plumber can locate the source, and that information helps whoever is responsible act quickly.

Know your shutoffs

In attached housing, find out which shutoff controls your unit. Apartments and townhomes may have an individual shutoff in a utility closet or at the water heater, while some buildings route water through a shared line. Ask your property manager or association where the shutoffs are and test them if you are allowed to.

If you rent here

Tenants should report leaks and no-hot-water problems to the landlord or property manager first, since they decide who to call. Owners and managers of multi-unit buildings can call us directly. Describe the building, the number of units affected and where the water is showing.

Homes, townhomes and apartments

For residents, the common calls are ordinary ones. A water heater that is slow to heat, a drain that backs up or a leak with an unclear source are typical examples. Leak detection helps most when the damage shows in one place and the water starts somewhere else.

Shops, restaurants and offices in the village core

The ULI case study says the village core includes shops, restaurants, offices and residences. Buildings like these have plumbing needs that differ from a house, including larger water heaters, floor drains and, in many commercial properties, backflow prevention assemblies. The water provider usually sets the testing schedule for those assemblies, so confirm yours with the city.

Our commercial plumbing and backflow testing pages describe what we handle. Describe the building and the problem on the call so the visit can be planned around it.

Legacy Ridge, a nearby golf-course community, has a different housing mix. See the Legacy Ridge page if that is closer to your address.

Before You Call: If Water Is Running

  1. Shut off the main water valve to stop the flow.
  2. Switch off power to any wet area only if you can reach the breaker without stepping through standing water.
  3. Move furniture, electronics and anything else that cannot get wet out of the area.

Common Questions

Bradburn Village Plumbing FAQ

Is Bradburn Village the same as Bradburn Boulevard?

No. Bradburn Village is the planned community along West 120th Avenue in north Westminster. Bradburn Boulevard is a different road, listed as the western edge of the historic Harris Park core in the city's Harris Park Community Vision Plan. They share a name but are miles apart.

How big is Bradburn Village?

The Urban Land Institute case study describes it as 123 acres, and a Westword article describes it as about 125 acres. The planned mix was more than 800 residences with retail and office space. Treat those as planned totals, because the finished community may differ.

Who is responsible for a leak between townhomes?

That depends on your association documents and on where the leak starts. Shut off your own water if it is running, and tell your association. A plumber can find the source, which makes it easier for the responsible party to act.

Do you take plumbing calls for shops and restaurants?

Our commercial plumbing page describes the work we handle. Call (303) 552-3896 and describe the building, the problem and any backflow or drain concerns, and the dispatcher can say what the next step is.

What is a live/work unit, and does it change the plumbing?

The ULI case study lists 33 live/work units, which combine living space with space for a small business. Plumbing in a unit like that may serve both uses, so tell the dispatcher how the space is used and which fixtures are involved. Who is responsible for which line still depends on the ownership and association documents.

How do I find out how often a backflow assembly must be tested?

Ask the City of Westminster's water utility, because the testing schedule is usually set by the water provider. Keep the test reports once they are done. Our backflow testing page describes what the testing involves, and a call lets you describe the building so the right assemblies are covered.

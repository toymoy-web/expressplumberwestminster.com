Tired of Repeated Pipe Leaks in the Same Old House?

A home that's had two or three unrelated leaks in the last couple of years, especially one with original galvanized supply lines, is usually looking at repiping, a full replacement of the home's supply lines rather than another one-off patch.

Signs a Home Has Outlived Its Original Piping

Galvanized steel supply lines corrode from the inside out over decades, which shows up as low water pressure, discolored water, and leaks that seem to appear in a new spot every year or two rather than staying fixed once patched.

Westminster's 14-story City Hall Bell Tower, a 1986 tribute to London's Big Ben, sits in the older core of town where a lot of the original 1960s and 1970s housing stock still runs its first set of supply lines.

What a Repipe Actually Involves

A full repipe runs new PEX or copper lines through the home, typically routed through walls, attic, or crawl space rather than replicating the old galvanized routing exactly, and includes patching drywall access points as part of the job.

Most whole-home repipes for a typical single-family house take 2 to 4 days, with water shut off in stages rather than for the entire job. All work is performed to the Colorado Plumbing Code, the state's IPC-based standard administered by DORA, with permits pulled and inspected as required.

Living in the House During the Work

Most repipe jobs let you stay in the home throughout, since water is shut off in stages, one bathroom or zone at a time, rather than for the entire house across all four days.

We patch drywall access points as part of the job, though painting and finish work is typically left to the homeowner or their preferred contractor, and we're upfront about that scope before the job starts.

Questions Worth Asking Before You Commit

Ask whether the quote includes drywall patching, what material is being used, and whether the job is staged to keep at least partial water access throughout, since these details vary meaningfully between contractors.

We answer all three plainly before any contract is signed, and walk the specific routing plan with you so there are no surprises about which walls get opened.

How the Job Is Staged

We walk the home together before quoting, mapping which walls and areas will need access, so there are no surprise openings once work begins that weren't discussed upfront.

Most single-family repipes run 2 to 4 working days, with water restored to at least part of the home each night rather than the whole house being without water for the full project.

Serving Westminster and Nearby

Repipe jobs concentrate in the pre-1980s housing near 80030 and 80221, where original galvanized supply lines are most common.

We're also happy to phase a repipe across a couple of visits for a household that needs to manage cash flow, prioritizing the highest-risk sections first rather than requiring the entire job upfront.

We test water pressure and flow at every fixture once a repipe is complete, confirming the improvement is real and even across the house, not just at the fixture closest to the main.

Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

  • Multiple unrelated pipe leaks in recent years
  • Low water pressure throughout the house
  • Rust-colored water from multiple fixtures
  • Original galvanized supply lines
  • Visible corrosion at exposed pipe joints

Common Questions

Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement FAQ

How do I know if my home needs repiping vs. spot repairs?

If leaks keep happening in different locations rather than the same spot, and the home still has original galvanized supply lines, spot repairs are usually a short-term fix on a system that's corroding uniformly. We'll camera-check accessible sections before recommending a full repipe over patch repairs.

Will a repipe increase my home's value?

It's not typically a renovation that shows visually, but it removes a real red flag for buyers and inspectors, particularly on a home with original galvanized lines, which can otherwise complicate a sale.

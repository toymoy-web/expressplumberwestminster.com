Basement Taking on Water When It Rains or Thaws?

A wet basement floor after heavy rain or a spring thaw, especially one with a sump pit that isn't keeping up, usually means it's time for sump pump installation or repair rather than just mopping it up and hoping the next storm is lighter.

Why Sump Pumps Matter More in Front Range Spring

Westminster's spring snowmelt, combined with occasional heavy rain, pushes groundwater up around foundations built into the area's clay-heavy soil, which drains slowly compared to sandier ground.

A sump pump that's undersized, aging, or has a failed check valve can't keep pace with a fast snowmelt, which is when basements flood even in homes that have never had a problem before.

Repair, Battery Backup, or Full Replacement

A pump that runs constantly, cycles oddly, or has a stuck float switch is usually repairable. A pump over 7 to 10 years old, or one that failed during an actual flood event, is worth replacing outright, and we strongly recommend a battery backup system for any home that's lost power during a storm before.

Sizing a Sump Pump Correctly

An undersized pump is one of the most common reasons a basement floods even with a working sump system. The pump has to be sized to the pit's actual inflow rate during a heavy snowmelt, not just a generic horsepower rating pulled off a shelf.

We size replacements based on the specific basement and pit, not a one-size-fits-all recommendation, which is part of why we check the existing pit and float switch before quoting a replacement.

Testing Your Pump Before You Need It

Pouring a few gallons of water into the pit and watching the pump engage and clear it is a genuinely useful test any homeowner can run before spring snowmelt, not just something to check after a flood.

A pump that hesitates, runs unusually loud, or doesn't fully clear the pit during that test is worth having looked at before the next real storm, not after.

Getting a Repair or Install Scheduled

An actively failing pump during wet weather gets emergency priority, since a basement flooding in real time isn't something that should wait for a standard appointment window.

A standard replacement, once the old pump and pit are inspected, typically takes under two hours including testing the new unit under load before we leave.

Primary vs. Backup Systems

A primary pump handles normal groundwater. A battery or water-powered backup takes over specifically during a power outage, which is exactly when a primary pump is most likely to be needed and least likely to be able to run.

Serving Westminster and Nearby

Sump pump calls concentrate in lower-lying and older basement construction near 80030 and 80221, where groundwater during spring snowmelt is more of a factor.

We test the full system, pump, float switch, and check valve together, since a replacement pump paired with a failing check valve can still let water flow backward into the pit between cycles.

Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

  • Water in the basement after rain or snowmelt
  • Sump pump runs constantly or not at all
  • Musty smell near the sump pit
  • Pump makes grinding or rattling noises
  • No battery backup during past power outages

Common Questions

Sump Pump Installation & Repair FAQ

Do I need a battery backup sump pump?

If your power has ever gone out during a heavy storm, a battery backup is worth the cost, since a primary pump that loses power during the exact event it's needed for defeats the purpose of having one.

How long do sump pumps typically last?

A standard pedestal or submersible pump usually lasts 7 to 10 years with normal use. A pump that's run through several major storm events without failing is usually near the end of that range regardless of its exact age.

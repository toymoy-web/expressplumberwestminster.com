Living With Westminster's Hard Water

Water delivered through Westy Water, sourced largely from Clear Creek, runs hard enough that most homes without a softener see scale buildup on fixtures, cloudy glassware, and shorter lifespans on water heaters and appliances that use hot water.

A softener that's stopped regenerating properly, is using excessive salt, or has a jammed valve stops protecting the home's plumbing even though the unit is technically still running.

Repair or Replace

Most softener problems, a stuck valve, a worn resin bed, a bridged salt tank, are repairable. A unit over 10 to 15 years old that's still underperforming after service is usually more cost-effective to replace outright with a more efficient system.

Salt-Based vs. Salt-Free Systems

A traditional salt-based softener is the most effective option against Westminster's harder water and is what we install and service most often. Salt-free conditioners exist but condition rather than fully soften, which is worth understanding before choosing one based on maintenance convenience alone.

We're upfront about that trade-off rather than upselling a salt-free system as a direct equivalent when it isn't, for a household dealing with genuinely hard water.

Signs the System Needs Attention Beyond Repair

A resin bed past 10 to 15 years of use gradually loses its softening capacity even if the unit is otherwise functioning, which shows up as hardness symptoms returning despite the system technically running.

We test water hardness directly rather than assuming, since a system that seems to be underperforming sometimes just needs a settings adjustment rather than a resin or full unit replacement.

What We Check During Service

We test actual water hardness before and after any softener service, rather than assuming the unit is working correctly just because it's running, since a system can operate without actually softening effectively.

A standard service or repair visit runs 30 minutes to an hour. A full new system installation typically takes about two hours including the initial setup and programming.

Where We Install the System

Most Westminster homes have room near the water heater or in a utility closet for a softener. We assess the actual plumbing layout during the quote rather than assuming a standard install location will work.

A tight utility space sometimes calls for a compact or dual-tank unit instead of a standard cabinet system, which we'll flag and price accordingly before installation day.

Serving Westminster and Nearby

Softener installs and service happen citywide, since the hard water itself comes through the same Westy Water system regardless of ZIP.

We also check your home's overall water pressure and supply line condition during a softener visit, since both interact with how well a softening system performs over its expected lifespan.

We walk through the salt-loading and basic maintenance routine with every new install, so the system keeps performing well between our regular service visits rather than relying on us to catch every issue. We're also glad to advise on bypass settings for outdoor spigots or irrigation, since softened water isn't necessary or cost-effective for every use around a property.

Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

  • Scale buildup on faucets and showerheads
  • Soap doesn't lather well
  • Water feels different after softener maintenance
  • Salt tank bridging or excessive usage
  • Softener not regenerating on schedule

Common Questions

Water Softener Installation & Repair FAQ

Is Westminster's water really that hard?

Water sourced through the Clear Creek watershed runs on the harder end for the Front Range, which is why softeners are common here even though they're not universal across every Colorado city.

How much salt does a typical softener use?

A standard household system uses roughly one 40-pound bag every 4 to 6 weeks depending on household size and water hardness, though usage varies enough that we'd rather check your specific system than guess.
